# Taste: the user's corrections

Rules learned from the user's own feedback. They beat the general guidance in core.md and the style files. Add a rule every time the user corrects a cut: what they asked for, and the rule behind it.

## All videos

- **Pace (Dan, head of marketing at Expo):** cut anything precious. A moment earns its place or it goes.
- **A strong intro.** Never open on logistics. Open on the premise, the stakes, or the best moment, and finish the hook in the first 30 seconds.
- **No product pitch that sounds like an ad,** unless it is funny (the 30-second pitch challenge was funny, so it stayed).

## Vlogs

- **Story over highlights.** The Hack the North recut built only from top-scored moments felt like a list. The earlier cut, built from a paper edit, felt like a story and was preferred. Always outline before selecting.
- **Keep the process.** When Rami announced free AirPods for joining the workshop, he wanted to show himself not knowing what to say and talking to the person filming him. Fumbles, asides to the camera person and figuring it out on the spot are content in a vlog: select them, mark them `raw`.
- **Suggest talking heads (2026-10-12, Mystery Interview).** Wherever a short line from Rami would clarify a jump or explain the lesson behind a moment, propose a talking head to record (`pickups`, core.md), so it shows on the review page's To record tab. Rami records them afterwards; the footage still carries the video.
- **Show the side nobody sees.** Rami's vlogs document what it is like on his side (the sponsor table, the creator's process), not the event itself.
- **Keep the run-up uncut.** When one clip ends and the next starts seconds later on the same moment, play them back to back with no trim: the end of the first clip to the start of the next, both raw. On the bad-advice vlog, Rami asked to remove the cut between "Bro, gimbal, follow me!" and "Feels a little awkward", and the cut between deciding to give bad advice and the first group he tried it on. The walk into an approach, and a plan going straight into its first try, are the courage: a cut there removes it. Check the capture times in the digest for clips that join this way, and write them as a continuous run in the story.

## Confirmed on the Hack the North cut (2026-10-12: "pretty good", approved with edits to come)

- The story-first structure: cold open on the premise line, a flash-forward of the weekend's best lines, title, chapters by day and place, an ending that answers "was it worth it?".
- Process moments played raw: the megaphone sequence from handing the camera to a stranger to "we got four people".
- Running threads: a gag planted early and paid off (the 3D printed logo), a person who returns (Ian), the hook's question answered at the end.
