# Taste: the user's corrections

Rules learned from the user's own feedback. They beat the general guidance in core.md and the style files. Add a rule every time the user corrects a cut: what they asked for, and the rule behind it.

## All videos

- **Pace (Dan, head of marketing at Expo):** cut anything precious. A moment earns its place or it goes. For a vlog this decides which scenes stay, never how much of a scene plays (see Vlogs, "Scenes play whole").
- **A strong intro.** Never open on logistics. Open on the premise, the stakes, or the best moment, and finish the hook in the first 30 seconds.
- **No product pitch that sounds like an ad,** unless it is funny (the 30-second pitch challenge was funny, so it stayed).

## Explainers

From the Expo team's notes on the SDK 58 video (2026-10-16).

- **Smash the highlights in the intro.** A release or feature video opens on its best moments, fast: one shot and one short phrase each, about 1.5 seconds per shot, before the first topic. Propose it as a voiceover pickup over shots from the cut.
- **The wrap-up repeats what matters.** End on the few things the viewer must remember (what is new, what is stable, how to upgrade), not "that is it, I could not cover everything".
- **Lead with what the viewer gets for free.** Frame a feature through the default the viewer already uses ("use Expo Router and you get this, pretty much for free"), then the specific component, with a niche package last. Check the live release post before writing lines: it changes after launch.

## Shorts

- **30 seconds to 3 minutes (2026-10-12, my story).** Aim for 30-90 seconds. Go up to 3 minutes only when the point needs the depth to land (a story whose payoff needs its setup). A clip longer than 3 minutes is not a short: find the strongest part inside it, or split it.
- **The v04 my-story picks failed as shorts.** As they played after the cleanup: 26 of 34 started mid-sentence or on words that need earlier context ("Which also, I forgot to talk about...", "So yeah, that was the peak experience. But another..."), 10 ended mid-sentence, and 12 were outside 30 seconds to 3 minutes. Read the first and last words that really play (after every cut) for every short, before it goes on the list.
- **Rami's own shorts: a headline the whole time, and the end card (2026-10-23, Hank Green reaction S7).** For a short cut from one of his long YouTube videos, the text on the finished short is always two things: a headline in his own words ("The power of communities") right under the black bar above the picture, on one line, from the first frame until the end card; and the "Watch the full video" end card with the long video's live thumbnail assembling, on the last phrase. Same Edits-style font as before (Barlow Condensed Bold, white, black outline). Propose the headline with the short; shorts-text renders both (`"top": "under-bar"`, `"until": "end"`, `"kind": "endcard"`, OpenMontage `.agents/skills/shorts-text/SKILL.md`). The Expo shorts keep their own text rules. Headlines Rami chose: "The power of communities" (S7), "Opportunities are EVERYWHERE" (S19: Rami's own wording of the short's point, not a quote, with one word in caps, on two lines) and "Ask better questions" (S16, from proposals). The end card now shows the long video's YouTube row under its thumbnail (live title, channel, avatar, duration): tried on S16, then Rami asked for it on S7 and S19 too, and "questions" lowercase in the S16 headline (one word in caps is not a rule). - **Scenes play whole (2026-10-21, Hack the North).** The 12-minute cut felt disconnected: 53 of its 78 selects were under 10 seconds, and 30 joins jumped forward inside one conversation, so clips started and stopped at odd times. Rami: "some clips deserve the full view time... keep the whole thing so people can be immersed in a specific scene", and "don't worry about duration: if a clip deserves its spot, keep it". A scene plays from where it starts to where it resolves; cut between scenes, not inside them. Every moment judged gets a verdict and a reason (the inclusion list), so he can bring back what was left out.
- **Whole scenes, but few of them (2026-10-21, Hack the North).** Taken literally, "don't worry about duration" made a 2-hour cut of 130 scenes, and Rami said "that is too long for a vlog... keep the highlights and remove the things that don't really need to be there... it has to be very selective". The all-scenes cut is the master, not the vlog. The vlog is a strict selection of whole scenes from it (30 minutes from the 2-hour master: 49 of 130 selects, 2 of 9 pitches). Shorten a long scene to one continuous core, never by cutting inside it. Tell him what was removed and why.
- **Story over highlights.** The Hack the North recut built only from top-scored moments felt like a list. The earlier cut, built from a paper edit, felt like a story and was preferred. Always outline before selecting.
- **Keep the process.** When Rami announced free AirPods for joining the workshop, he wanted to show himself not knowing what to say and talking to the person filming him. Fumbles, asides to the camera person and figuring it out on the spot are content in a vlog: select them, mark them `raw`.
- **Suggest talking heads (2026-10-12, Mystery Interview).** Wherever a short line from Rami would clarify a jump or explain the lesson behind a moment, propose a talking head to record (`pickups`, core.md), so it shows on the review page's To record tab. Rami records them afterwards; the footage still carries the video.
- **Show the side nobody sees.** Rami's vlogs document what it is like on his side (the sponsor table, the creator's process), not the event itself.
- **Never play a covered lens (2026-10-26, free pics).** On the free-photo-shoot vlog, in the handheld stretches the FREE PICS foam poster covered the lens (or the camera hung against the jacket of the person filming) while the words played on. Rami: "the camera's just not showing anything, it's being covered by the cardboard. Be mindful of that". The transcript cannot see this. Before the scenes are filled, measure every keyframe for a flat, featureless frame (on 192x108 gray frames, 12 px blocks with a standard deviation under 6 on 60% of the frame or more, and edge energy under 9, caught every poster frame), check each flagged stretch inside a story beat by eye and at 4 frames per second, and find the jacket stretches by eye (they have texture, the measure misses them). Write the stretches to `covered.md` and give them to the fill agents as a fixed rule: a select never plays more than about 1 second of covered lens. A covered stretch inside a scene is cut out (a jump); a line lost that way is named, so Rami can bring it back with a cutaway in Final Cut.
- **Speed through, do not cut, the behind the scenes (2026-11-15, ChatGPT vs Claude shirt).** Rami: "for boring parts that would be good to show the behind the scenes of, we can do a speed section, times four", and "the small talk can also be sped up x4". The priority is entertainment. A stretch that shows the process but drags (setting up, small talk, a long explanation, a pitch) plays as its own select with `"speed": 4`, raw, with the sound sped up too (not muted, not under music). Keep these sections short in play time (about 15-40 s each): the viewer sees it happened and moves on. A stretch with nothing worth seeing is still cut.
- **Keep the run-up uncut.** When one clip ends and the next starts seconds later on the same moment, play them back to back with no trim: the end of the first clip to the start of the next, both raw. On the bad-advice vlog, Rami asked to remove the cut between "Bro, gimbal, follow me!" and "Feels a little awkward", and the cut between deciding to give bad advice and the first group he tried it on. The walk into an approach, and a plan going straight into its first try, are the courage: a cut there removes it. Check the capture times in the digest for clips that join this way, and write them as a continuous run in the story.
